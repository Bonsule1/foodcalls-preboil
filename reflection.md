# Reflection

*My points, from what I told the AI in chat; it only turned them into simple English.*

**What I got wrong at the start**
At first I wanted to solve the seating problem. FoodCalls has only 18 seats, and when the after-class rush comes, people have to be turned away. But seating is a space and rent problem. I can't count my way to more chairs, and a script can't create space. So I switched to the boiling problem: noodles and pasta are boiled in advance, the leftovers get thrown away, and the quantity is a daily guess. That happens every day, costs him money, and can be helped with numbers. I also planned to count real sales, but I couldn't go back, so my data is an estimate built from what Nivesh ji told me.

**What I would cut**
I wrote down every problem he mentioned: GST, food licence, pest control, online orders, electricity. It was interesting, but most of it was useless for this assignment. Next time I would ask fewer general questions and spend that time sitting through one 5-8 pm rush, counting plates. Real counts of even one evening would be worth more than all those notes.

**Where AI helped and where it wasted time**
The part that mattered most was mine: going to the shop, talking to Nivesh ji, taking the photos and notes, and deciding which problem was worth solving. AI helped me write the Python script and the README, read the two sources, and keep the files tidy. It wasted time on the dataset. I kept asking it to make up counted data, it kept refusing, and we went back and forth before settling on an honestly labelled estimate. Looking back, that time would have been better spent at the shop counting.
